## 3. What marked the window — the clue

Tested every minute of the day (n=2568 option-minutes, 8 strikes,
09:20–15:20) against the option's own forward 15- and 30-minute return.

Baseline for the day: avg fwd15 **-0.4%**, 39% positive. Decay wins by
default, as expected on expiry.

Filters, each added on top of the last:

| Filter | n | avg fwd15 | avg fwd30 | win (30m) |
|--------|---|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| everything | 2568 | -0.4% | +2% | 31% |
| option volume ≥ 2x its own 20-min median | 225 | +5.6% | | 46% |
| + index 10-min efficiency ≥ 60, net ≥ 40 pts, aligned with the leg | 70 | +22.3% | | 60% |
| **+ index at a NEW SESSION EXTREME** | **33** | **+45.3%** | **+100%** | **78%** |
| + option not already > 2x off its own 60-min low | **14** | +48% | **+172%** | **93%** |

Same base filter but **not** at a new session extreme: n=37, avg fwd15
**+1.7%**, 46% win. That is the whole signal in one comparison.

### The four conditions

1. **Index at a new session high (for CE) or new session low (for PE).**
   This is the single biggest discriminator in the data. At 13:19 the index
   printed 79119 against a prior session high of **79111** — it cleared the
   day's high by 8 points and then ran 346.
2. **Index 10-min efficiency ≥ 60% and net ≥ 40 points**, direction matching
   the leg. Through the run, efficiency sat 64–82.
3. **Option minute volume ≥ 2x its own 20-minute median.** At 13:19 79800CE
   traded 604k against a ~290k median. At 13:38 it traded 2,339k — 3.9x.
4. **Option not already more than 2x off its own 60-minute low.** Fresh, not
   chased. Signals passing 1–3 but already > 2x extended returned +43% over
   30 min at a 67% hit rate; those under 2x returned **+172% at 93%**.

### Open interest — read it correctly

OI falling on its own is a *bad* sign: dOI(5m) < 0 scored avg fwd15 **-3.7%**
across the day, worse than the -0.4% baseline. It mostly just marks decay.

OI only matters **in combination with a volume spike**:

| | n | avg fwd15 | win |
|---|---|-----------|-----|
| vol ≥ 3x and dOI(5m) < -2% | 24 | +14.8% | 63% |
| vol ≥ 3x, dOI < -2%, aligned | 22 | +18.0% | 68% |

And in the leg itself the sequence is clean. 79800CE open interest:

- 13:01 → 13:37: OI **rises** 2,156k → 2,747k. Writers are still selling into
  the move. Price only crawls 10.85 → 19.10.
- 13:38: index +68 points in one minute, volume 2,339k (3.9x), OI **drops**
  2,747k → 2,570k. Writers are covering.
- 13:38 → 13:48: OI falls to 2,410k. Price goes 25.05 → 53.90. This is the
  vertical part.
- 13:51 → 13:54: OI **rises again** 2,410k → 2,790k while price falls 62.65 →
  46.10. Writers are back. The move is over.

So: **rising OI + rising price = grind. Falling OI + volume spike =
vertical. Rising OI + falling price = done, get out.**

### What the filter got wrong

Only two false-positive clusters survived all four conditions, and both are
worth naming:

- **09:52** (79500CE @ 64.80, fwd15 -41.7%). Index +165, efficiency 79, new
  high by 32 points — looked identical. But the option was already 4x off its
  session low, and the "session high" reference was only 37 minutes old. A
  new-extreme test needs a reference window with some age in it.
- **13:01** (five strikes, fwd15 -1.5% to -20.5%). A 20-point break of the
  high that was rejected within a minute (13:02 index -54). fwd30 was mostly
  positive — it was early, not wrong. The 13:19 break of the *same* level is
  the one that held.

Consecutive volume did **not** help as a confirmation (requiring 4 minutes of
≥1.5x volume dropped avg fwd30 from +100% to +24%). By the time the volume
has been elevated for four minutes, the move is already paid out. Take the
first spike.

## 5. Why we got shaken out — the trail, not the breach

This is the headline finding and it is a code problem, not a judgement one.

`TRAIL_CLEAR_PCT = 0.5` (chartApp.js:907). `maybeTrail` (chartApp.js:3216)
walks a ladder of resistance levels, and the moment LTP clears a level by
**0.5%**, that level becomes the stop:

```js
if (ltp >= L * (1 + TRAIL_CLEAR_PCT / 100)) lv = L;   // 0.5%
...
st.stop = lv;  breachLevelByToken.set(t, lv);
```

Now put that against what one minute of these options actually does.
Measured from today's tape, 09:30–15:00:

| Strike | median 1m body | p90 body | median 1m high-low range |
|--------|----------------|----------|--------------------------|
| 79800CE | 4.5% | 14.1% | 9.9% |
| 79500CE | 5.0% | 13.6% | 10.2% |
| 80000CE | 4.3% | 12.8% | 9.0% |
| 80300CE | 3.6% | 12.6% | 7.4% |

And **inside the 13:19–13:50 leg**:

| Strike | median 1m range | p90 1m range |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| 79800CE | **15.3%** | **38.0%** |
| 80300CE | 14.8% | 38.3% |
| 80000CE | 16.3% | 36.5% |
| 79500CE | 13.4% | 30.7% |

The trail gives the trade **0.5%** of room where a normal minute is **15%**
and a 90th-percentile minute is **38%**. The stop is roughly **30x tighter
than one minute of ordinary noise.** It is inside the bid-ask spread.

That is exactly what the log shows:

- Trade 2: entry 12.61 → price clears 13.80 → stop snaps to 13.80 → out at
  13.70, **20 seconds**.
- Trade 3: entry 13.50 → price clears 16.70 → stop snaps to 16.70 → out at
  16.35, **36 seconds**.

On a vertical leg, every resistance level is cleared in turn and each one
instantly becomes a zero-room stop. The faster the move, the faster the exit.
The trail is anti-correlated with the thing we want to catch.

## 7. The gates that blocked the window

Blocker counts across today's 102 setups (84 BLOCKED, 15 THIN, **3 TAKE**):

```
 31  PE needs the index biased down
 24  Index not chopping (30-min efficiency >= 10%)
 21  Premium ₹8–400
 17  No entry 12:00–13:15 (low volume)
 16  No entry for 10 min after a stop-out
 15  CE needs the index biased up
 13  Last 1m candle under 2.5x its own median body
  6  Option price >= ₹3
  5  No entry after 15:10 (low volume)
  4  Current 1m candle not red
```

The regime filter did its job — it is not on this list as a problem. Three
gates misfired on the one window that mattered:

**7.1 The 10-minute stop-out lockout.** `STOP_REASONS = ['sl_hit',
'guard_breach']` (chartApp.js:1113). Trades 2 and 3 exited on `guard_breach`
**while green** (+8.7%, +21.1%) and each armed a 10-minute lockout. That
lockout then blocked re-entry at 13:30 (12.20), 13:33 (13.90) and **13:42
(34.00)** — the entire run. A breach that fills above entry is not a
stop-out.

**7.2 `minPremium: 8`.** Blocked 21 setups, including 80300CE at 13:42 @ 6.45
and 80000CE at 13:12 @ 5.75. Those two were the day's **7.36x and 7.31x**.
The premium floor excludes precisely the population that multiplies.

**7.3 `minRR: 1.5` on the OI path** (chartApp.js:2823, 2972). Skipped 13:26
79800CE @ 14.00 with `rr 0.4 under 1.5` — that minute returned **+105% in 15
minutes and +229% in 30**. RR is measured to the nearest resistance, and a
multiplier is by definition a move that goes *through* resistance. In a
breakout this filter is inverted.

**7.4 `blockRedCandle`** blocked 13:30 with "Current 1m candle not red". In a
vertical leg there are no red candles. Waiting for one means waiting for the
move to end.

## 8. Changes, in order of what they would have paid today

1. **Raise `TRAIL_CLEAR_PCT` and make it adaptive.** Require price to clear a
   ladder level by at least **1x the option's own median 1-minute high-low
   range** over the last 20 bars (≈10% quiet, ≈15% in a leg), floored at the
   current 0.5% only for genuinely dead strikes. This is the single change
   that turns a 36-second exit into a 19-minute hold on the same entry.
2. **Do not arm the 10-min lockout when the exit filled above entry.** Gate
   on `sell < buy`, not on `reason`.
3. **Widen the breach to a 6-candle low while the leg is live** (index
   efficiency ≥ 60 and at a session extreme); keep 3 candles for chop. The
   5→6 cliff in section 5 is the whole difference between 1.04x and 4.33x.
4. **Drop `minPremium` to 3 when the multiplier conditions are on**, 8
   otherwise. The 7x strikes started at 2.75 and 5.65.
5. **Suspend `minRR` and `blockRedCandle` while at a new session extreme with
   efficiency ≥ 60.** Both gates assume mean reversion.
6. **Add the "already run" cap: refuse any entry more than 2x off the option's
   own 60-minute low.** This refuses trade 4 and costs nothing elsewhere — in
   today's data the > 2x bucket returned +43%/67% against +172%/93% under 2x.
7. **Add a new-session-extreme watcher.** When spot prints a new session
   high/low with 10-min efficiency ≥ 60 and net ≥ 40 points, alert on the
   200–1200 point OTM strikes on that side whose minute volume is ≥ 2x their
   own 20-min median. That is the entire signal.
8. **Size down as the leg matures, not up.** Trade 4 was the largest position
   of the day at the worst moment.

## 9. Honest caveats

- This is **one expiry day**, n=14 signals at the tightest filter. The
  thresholds (2x volume, eff 60, 40 points, 2x extended) are fitted to it.
  Re-run section 3 over 29/10, 28/10 and the other logged expiries before
  trusting the numbers.
- fwd30 of +172% assumes a 30-minute hold. With today's trail that hold is
  impossible, so fix 8.1 before believing any of it.
- Expiry-day gamma is doing most of the work in the OTM multiples. The same
  filter on a non-expiry day will find much smaller moves.
- First-minute prints on expiry strikes are garbage (section 2). Skip the
  09:15 bar in anything that computes a session extreme or a median.

## 10. Summary

The day had one leg: 13:19 to 13:48, +346 index points, and 7x in the OTM
calls. The clue that marked it was mechanical and visible in real time — the
index broke the session high (79111 → 79119), 10-minute efficiency was 66,
and the cheap calls lit up on 2–4x their own normal volume while open
interest dropped as writers covered.

We were in the right strike at 13:23 and 13:34 and held for 56 seconds,
because a 0.5% trail is 30x tighter than one minute of that option's normal
movement. Our own green exits then armed a 10-minute lockout that fenced off
the rest of the run, and the ₹8 premium floor had already hidden the two
strikes that did 7.3x. We finished +₹199 and gave back ₹888 buying the same
leg four minutes after it topped, in double the size.

Nothing about finding the move was wrong. Everything about holding it was.
