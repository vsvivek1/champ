minutes. The far calls in that same window:

| Strike | 13:36 | peak | multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| 79200CE | 146.65 | 348.45 | 2.4x |
| 79800CE | 14.60 | 64.60 | 4.4x |

From 13:19, which is where the first clear tells were already in place,

The move was not a surprise. It was telegraphed for about 40 minutes, and

## 1. Higher lows for a full hour. The simplest tell of all.

The 15-minute low never once made a lower low from 12:33 to 13:33:

    12:33  78887
    12:50  78921
    12:58  78932
    13:14  79006
    13:27  79069
    13:33  79111

Six steps up, no step down. Every pullback was bought higher than the last
one. This started an hour before the move and it needed no volume data, no
OI, nothing — only the spot candles the app already keeps.

The app has a `higherHighs()` helper, but it only runs on the **option**,
over 3 bars. Nothing watches higher lows on the **spot**.

## 2. The squeeze. Range got tight right before it expanded.

15-minute spot width:

    12:37  119 points
    12:50   85
    12:58   64    <- tightest
    13:19  113
    13:38  178
    13:48  296

1-minute ranges in the quiet patch (12:41-12:47) were 22, 18, 17, 16, 33,
13, 23 points. After 13:37 they were 49, 78, 30, 15, 26, 38, 48.

Tight range, then expansion. The squeeze bottomed at 12:58 and the first
leg started on the very next bar (12:59 close 79014, a new day high).

The app has no squeeze detector at all.

## 3. The efficiency and path numbers the app ALREADY computes.

`computeRegime()` works out `net` and `path` over 30 minutes and then only
reports `eff = net/path`. Look at what `path` alone was doing:

    13:02  eff 23%  net +117  path 509
    13:19  eff 31%  net +135  path 441
    13:30  eff 27%  net  +96  path 351
    13:35  eff 60%  net +169  path 280   <- two minutes before the blow-off

Path fell 45% from 13:02 to 13:35 while `bias` stayed `up` the whole time.
That is the market doing the same distance with far less back-and-forth.
It is a textbook coil, and the app computes both halves of it already — it
simply throws `path` away and keeps the ratio.

Note also: `bias` read **up at every single minute from 12:30 to 13:50**.
There was never a wrong-way moment to be confused by.

And the trap: `blockChop` uses `minEff 10`. Between 12:51 and 12:57 the
efficiency was 9, 4, 11, 7, 9, 4, 3 — so the chop gate was **blocking
entries at the exact moment the coil was tightest**. The quietest part of a
squeeze looks identical to chop if all you measure is efficiency.

## 4. The call/put volume ratio. The best single number, and the app destroys it.

Calls and puts, total units per minute across the eight tracked strikes:

| Time | call vol | put vol | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12:30-12:58 | ~1.0-3.5M | ~0.4-2.2M | 1.4 to 2.9 (normal) |
| 13:02 | 6.19M | 1.42M | 4.36 |
| 13:03 | 4.67M | 0.96M | 4.89 |
| 13:17 | 3.38M | 0.59M | 5.73 |
| 13:19 | 5.43M | 0.69M | **7.89** |
| 13:22 | 4.83M | 0.60M | 8.10 |
| 13:25 | 4.38M | 0.48M | 9.19 |
| 13:26 | 4.80M | 0.43M | 11.19 |
| 13:37 | 9.71M | 0.67M | 14.56 |

From 13:22 the ratio never dropped below 4 again. It was sitting between 8
and 11 for a **full 15 minutes** before the explosion at 13:37.

Two separate things were happening, and both matter:

- **Calls were busy.** Not extreme — 1.3 to 2.0x their own 20-bar median at
  13:17-13:24. A volume filter alone would have shrugged at this.
- **Puts were empty.** 0.58 to 0.72x their median at 13:16-13:26. This is
  the half that actually carried the information. Nobody wanted the other
  side.

The ratio catches it because it divides one by the other. A one-sided book
is what a move needs.

**`participation()` folds CE and PE together on purpose.** That is correct
for asking "is this strike dead", but it deletes exactly this signal. The
app has never once looked at calls against puts.

## 5. Option OI. Three different stories, all pointing the same way.

**Puts were being abandoned, steadily, from 12:17.**

    78200PE  12:30 29.97 lakh -> 13:19 21.01 -> 13:40 16.12   (-46%)
    78300PE  12:30 24.70      -> 13:19 20.05 -> 13:40 17.96   (-27%)
    78400PE  12:30 34.44      -> 13:19 30.25 -> 13:24 22.98    (-33%)

The 78400PE print at 13:21 was **−19.5% in one step**. Put holders gave up
in a single move, 16 minutes before the explosion.

**Far calls were being quietly accumulated for an hour while their price
did nothing.**

    79800CE  12:00 19.20 lakh -> 13:35 27.48   (+43%), price stayed 8.5-10.8
    80300CE  12:00 12.50      -> 13:13 11.97   (flat), price stayed 2.75-3.9

79800CE is the striking one: OI up 43% with the price flat. Somebody was
loading up and the price was not telling anyone.

**Call sellers started covering, and that is the squeeze itself.**

    79200CE  13:16 -1.1%  13:19 -6.8%   <- the first run
             13:35 -6.0%  13:38 -7.7%
             13:40 -25.6%  13:43 -23.5%  13:48 -20.3%   <- the squeeze

The pre-sign is the 13:19 print of −6.8% while price was rising. That is
the existing OI signal's logic (OI down, price up) and it fired — but it
was skipped. See the note at the end.

## 6. Day highs kept getting taken, and not one was given back.

New day highs: 79006 (12:37), 79019 (12:59), 79051, 79071 (13:01), 79082,
79098, 79100, 79104, 79111, 79125, 79149, 79161 (13:24), 79192 (13:31).

Thirteen new highs in 54 minutes. Not one of them was rejected back inside
the old range. A failed breakout gives the level back within a few bars;
none of these did. The deepest pullback in the whole hour was 79161 →
79111, which is 50 points, and it held above the previous day high.

## 7. The volume burst at 13:00 that did not get sold back.

Call volume 6.16M at 13:00 and 5.67M at 13:01 — 4.4x and 4.1x the 20-bar
median, the first real burst of the afternoon. Spot ran 78983 → 79071, then
pulled back only to 79016 and stopped.

A big burst that gets sold straight back is distribution. A big burst that
holds is accumulation. This one held for 35 minutes before going again.

## What this means for the app

Ranked by how much each one would have been worth on this day:

1. **Watch calls against puts, not calls plus puts.** A per-minute CE/PE
   volume ratio on the running strikes. On this day it broke 4 at 13:02 and
   held above 8 from 13:22 — 15 minutes of warning for a 4-6x move. This is
   the single biggest gap and the data is already in `secCandles`.
2. **Watch put OI falling.** 78200PE lost 30% of its OI between 12:30 and
   13:19. Options OI is already polled every 3 minutes for the OI signal.
3. **Higher lows on the spot, not just on the option.** Six higher lows in
   an hour, using candles the app already has.
4. **Keep `path`, not only `eff`.** Falling path with `bias` up is the coil.
   `computeRegime()` computes it and discards it.
5. **A squeeze flag.** 15-minute spot width at a session low, and then
   expanding. Width hit 64 at 12:58 against 119 twenty minutes earlier.
6. **Do not let `blockChop` veto a tight coil.** eff was 3-9% at 12:51-12:57
   because the range was tight, not because the market was undecided —
   `bias` was up and the 15-minute low was rising. Low efficiency plus a
   shrinking range plus rising lows is the opposite of chop.
7. **A day-high streak counter.** Thirteen new highs, zero given back.

## One more thing about this exact window

The signs were there and the app saw some of them. It was the gates that
refused, not the eyes:

- The 79200CE OI signal (OI down, price up) fired in this window and was
  **skipped on `minRR 1.5`** — 16 such skips today. On a strike making new
  highs the nearest resistance is just overhead, so R:R always reads tiny
  exactly when the move is strongest.
- The cheap far calls that went 4-6x were blocked **22 times** on the
  `Premium ₹8-400` floor. 80300CE was refused at 6.80 and again at 3.20. It
  reached 12.80. That floor is now 2 on disk.
- The 13:30, 13:33 and 13:42 re-entries were refused by the **10-minute
  lockout** after two GREEN guard breaches. That is what `claudeLock.js`
  was built for.
